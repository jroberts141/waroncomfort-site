“For it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich person to enter the kingdom of God.’ … But he said, “What is impossible with man is possible with God.””

Luke 18:25, 27

War on Comfort equips Christians and their churches to leave comfort for covenant, doing hard things together until we depend on Jesus for everything. Not because hard things are holy. Because comfort quietly trains us to lean on ourselves, and hard things train us to lean on Christ.

We’re building it now, starting with a small group at our own church. When it’s ready, you’ll hear about it here first.

Two roads

Matthew 7:13-14. Comfort is the first step down a wide road. The way back up starts with Christ.

The wide road · Consumerism

  1. Comfort“I’m fine as I am.”
  2. Complacency“It can wait.”
  3. Compromise“Just this once.”
  4. Complicity“Everyone else is.”

The narrow road · Covenant

  1. Commission“Go. I am with you.”
  2. Commitment“Even when it costs.”
  3. Conviction“I can’t stay here.”
  4. Connection“I need Christ and His people.”

Consumer or covenant?

From thread to cord

Three steps, each built on the last, and each open to you, your group, or your whole church.

Step 1 · Days to a weekend

Hard Things

The 14-Day Fast, the New Year Plunge in early January and an outdoor retreat. Do one or all, on your own, with a group, or with your church.

Step 2 · 6 weeks

The Narrow Road

A six-week Bible study on doing hard things for the sake of Christ, with a video for every session. Editions for men, couples, teens, and families.

Step 3 · All year

Cord

Groups that keep going, all year, with strategic and tactical resources for church leaders: a field guide, Hard Day event plans, and leader training.

War on Comfort started in a church service with the convicting question of who among us had become victims of lukewarm living. Why do we go to church if not to let the Bible challenge us and make us uncomfortable? That question started a journey: firing up my faith, living more intentionally in community, and waging war on comfort. My family and I are on a quest to do hard things that deepen our dependence on Jesus. We want to reach the end knowing we gave all we had and to stand humbly and faithfully, waiting to hear, “Well done, good and faithful servant.”Jeremiah, founder
