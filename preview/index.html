The enemy

Comfort is the enemy

Comfort is quiet. It tells us we have enough, know enough, and are doing enough. It lets us coast through the Christian life on our own resources until we forget we ever needed God at all.

Comfort isn't a sin. A warm house, a full fridge, and money in the bank are good gifts. The enemy is anything that becomes our master in Jesus' place.

Jesus watched a rich young ruler walk away sad, and said it is easier for a camel to go through a needle's eye than for a rich man to enter the kingdom of God. Then He said the part we need most: what is impossible for people is possible for God (Luke 18:27).

We can't thread that needle. He can. So we pick a fight with the comfort that keeps us from Him.

Why we fight comfort

The story behind the name

The eye of the needle

In Luke 18 a rich young ruler asks Jesus what he must do to inherit eternal life. He has kept the commandments from his youth. Jesus answers him plainly.

When Jesus heard this, he said to him, “One thing you still lack. Sell all that you have and distribute to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me.” Luke 18:22 (ESV)

The man walks away very sorrowful, for he was very rich. Then Jesus says the line that has unsettled comfortable people ever since.

For it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich person to enter the kingdom of God.” Luke 18:25 (ESV)

The disciples are stunned and ask who then can be saved. His answer is the whole gospel in a sentence.

But he said, “What is impossible with man is possible with God.” Luke 18:27 (ESV)

The point was never to squeeze the camel through. We can't get through on our money, our comfort, or our effort. Only God brings a sinner through, by grace. The needle's eye is the picture of that, and it is why this company exists.

Why comfort?

Our kind of riches

Most of us will never be tempted to trust in gold. We are tempted to trust in comfort: a full refrigerator, a steady paycheck, a calendar with no room for God to interrupt. The rich fool in Luke 12 said it out loud.

And I will say to my soul, “Soul, you have ample goods laid up for many years; relax, eat, drink, be merry.”’ Luke 12:19 (ESV)

Comfort is a good gift and a terrible master. It persuades us we don't need daily bread from anyone.

Good gifts make terrible masters. We leave comfort to follow Jesus, not because comfort is a sin. Paul didn't tell the rich to stop being rich; he told them not to set their hope on riches, and to be generous and ready to share (1 Timothy 6:17-19).

So we fight it. We don't fight to earn our way to God, which is impossible. We fight the way Paul told Timothy to, training ourselves for godliness, putting ourselves where the Lord has promised to meet His people: in His Word, in prayer, in fasting, in giving, and in the fellowship of the local church.

And he said to all, “If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me. Luke 9:23 (ESV)

What we believe

Followers of Jesus

We are followers of Jesus Christ, and we gladly serve any church that preaches the gospel. These convictions shape everything we write.

  • The Bible is our final authority. All Scripture is given by inspiration of God and is sufficient for faith and practice (2 Timothy 3:16). Every program we publish starts and ends in the text.
  • Salvation is by grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone. "For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast." (Ephesians 2:8-9). Fasting earns nothing. Discipline is training, never merit.
  • The local church comes first. We serve churches; we don't replace them. Baptism and the Lord's Supper belong to the gathered church. Our day 14 meal is a fellowship meal, and we encourage every participant to walk the fast under the care of their own pastor.
  • Fasting is voluntary and done for the Father. Jesus said "when you fast," and told us to do it without show (Matthew 6:16-18). No one is pressured, ranked, or praised for the level they choose.
  • Every believer is a priest before God. Each Christian answers to the Lord for his own walk. We offer tools and brothers and sisters to walk beside you, not a rule to bind your conscience.
  • What we're given, we give. The ruler wasn't turned away for being rich. He walked away because his wealth had become his master. We give away a tenth of every sale and publish where it goes.

Who we are

It started in a pew

War on Comfort began on a Sunday morning in Coweta County, Georgia, with a Bible sliding off one knee and a notebook off the other. The first idea was simple: a better way to hold the Word and take notes at the same time.

The harder question came next. Why make Bible study easier if we aren't going to let the Bible make us uncomfortable? That question turned a product into a mission, and a season of studying biblical fasting turned the mission into the 14-Day Fast.

Why fast?

Hunger that teaches

When Jesus taught on fasting He didn't say "if." He said "when you fast." Moses, David, Daniel, Esther, Anna, Paul, and the church at Antioch all fasted. It is one of the ordinary means God uses to humble His people and turn their hearts to Him.

Fasting takes away one of our deepest comforts so we can feel, for a few weeks, how much we depend on Him. It isn't a diet, a detox, or a test of willpower. It doesn't make God love you more. It is a way of saying with your body what you believe with your heart: Lord, You are enough.

And he humbled you and let you hunger and fed you with manna, which you did not know, nor did your fathers know, that he might make you know that man does not live by bread alone, but man lives by every word that comes from the mouth of the LORD.Deuteronomy 8:3 (ESV)

Fast safely

Read this before you sign up. Fasting from food is not safe for everyone. Talk with your doctor before you begin any level that changes what you eat.

Choose the Prayer fast if you

  • are pregnant or nursing
  • are under 18 (the Prayer fast is the only level open to you)
  • have diabetes, or a heart, kidney, or liver condition
  • have ever had an eating disorder
  • take prescription medication, unless your doctor approves another level

At every level

  • Drink water through the day.
  • Stop and get medical help if you faint, feel confused, have chest pain, or notice a racing or irregular heartbeat.
  • Come back to food slowly. Follow the return plan in your level guide.
  • Moving to a gentler level is not failing. You are still in the fast, and your seat at the table is still yours.

The Water fast level is open only to adults who send us written approval from their own physician before day 1. We recommend bloodwork before you begin, coordinated through your doctor, along with any check-ins your doctor sets. We will move anyone to another level if written approval isn't received.

Under 18? The Prayer fast is the only level open to anyone under 18, with a parent or guardian’s permission. It is a full part of the fast, with the same seat at the table.

This program is a spiritual discipline, not medical treatment. Nothing on this site is medical advice or a substitute for the care of your physician. We make no claims about physical results.

A War on Comfort Bible study · Matthew 7:14

The Narrow Road

Six weeks. Six hard things. One point: the hard thing isn’t holy. It trains us to be all right when we’re uncomfortable, so we can hand everything over to Jesus.

Bring it to your groupSee the editions

Why hard things

Training, not earning

Nothing in this study makes God love you more. If you are in Christ, you are accepted by grace through faith. But comfort trains us every day to settle, and someone who can’t stand being uncomfortable can’t follow Jesus very far.

So each week has one hard thing: a quiet evening with the screens off, an early hour, a day of going without, a gift you feel, an apology with no “but,” and serving someone who can’t repay you. Each one is practice at leaning on Him.

And he said to all, “If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me.Luke 9:23 (ESV)

How it works

Six weeks, one road

  1. A weekly session with a short video, the passage, discussion, and prayer.
  2. Four days of work between sessions: three short days in the Word, and a fourth day for the week’s hard thing.
  3. A partner who checks in with you midweek.
  4. A capstone after week six: one larger hard thing, planned and done together.

Five editions

The same road, walked where you are

For Men

Six hard things for men who want to rely on Jesus, not on themselves. Includes a one-day fast.

For Couples

Walked side by side, with other couples. Pray aloud together, give together, and ask each other’s forgiveness.

For Teens

Plain language and real challenges, done with a parent’s knowledge. A screen fast instead of a food fast.

For Families

Parent-led nights around the table for families with young children, with an object lesson every week. No one skips a meal.

A Women’s edition is in the works.

Why ice water

One minute of choosing Him

The cold isn’t holy. It’s one minute of practice at choosing Christ over comfort, done together at the start of a new year. Jesus didn’t say “if it feels right.” He said daily.

And he said to all, “If anyone would come after me, let him deny himself and take up his cross daily and follow me.Luke 9:23 (ESV)

Up to one minute in the water counts. So does thirty seconds. Wives, kids, and friends are welcome to watch from the warming area, and anyone can choose to serve as a spotter instead.

The night

About 90 minutes, start to finish

Early January, on a Friday night. Date and time to be announced.

StartArrive, sign in, and sign the acknowledgment. Change inside.
+15 minA ten-minute devotion while everyone is still warm.
+30 minSafety talk. Tanks and order assigned.
+45 minEveryone at the tanks. Count down together.
+46 minThe first person in each tank. One in, one on deck, one heading to warm up.
+70 minLast one out. Towels, blankets, coffee, and cocoa.
+80 minClosing prayer and a card to take home with the fast's start date.

Read this first

Safety

  • If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, or another medical concern, sit out or get your doctor’s okay first.
  • No alcohol, before or during. We say it plainly.
  • Walk in slowly. Never jump. The danger is the shock of sudden cold, which makes people gasp and breathe fast.
  • No heads under water. One spotter on every tank, watching the whole time and calling time.
  • Out at one minute, or sooner if you’re shaking hard, confused, or can’t control your breathing.
  • Someone trained in first aid on site, and someone who knows the way to the nearest emergency room.

Don’t plunge alone, at home, or in open water. Do it at an organized event with spotters.

For churches

Host one in your parking lot

It takes an afternoon of setup and a few hundred dollars, and the tanks last for years. The host kit includes:

  • The full event plan: schedule, roles, supplies, and ballpark costs
  • A safety briefing script and the participant acknowledgment
  • The ten-minute devotion on Luke 9:23
  • Take-home cards that point everyone to the 14-Day Fast

Plan on one 100-gallon stock tank for every 8 to 10 people, and about 80 to 100 pounds of ice per tank.

Hard Things · One weekend · Mark 1:35

The Outdoor Retreat

Friday night to Sunday noon, outside, with the phones left at the gate. A dawn climb, an hour alone with God, shared work, and a fire. You come home tired, quieter, and closer to the people you went with.

Get the datesSee the weekend

Why outside

Out where comfort can’t follow

Jesus went out to solitary places to pray. He took His friends up mountains and out on the water. Away from screens, schedules, and soft chairs, we notice how much we lean on comfort, and how much He is enough without it.

And rising very early in the morning, while it was still dark, he departed and went out to a desolate place, and there he prayed.Mark 1:35 (ESV)

Who it’s for

Four ways to go

  • Men. The men’s spring retreat, ending with Ebenezer Night.
  • Couples. Separate tents for the solitary hour, one fire at night.
  • Fathers and sons. A shorter climb, sized for the youngest boy.
  • A whole Cord. Take your group out for its own weekend.

The weekend

Gather, Word, the hard thing, send

Friday evening

Leave it at the gate

Arrive by 6:00 p.m. and drop your phone in the box. Set up camp together, cook supper over the fire, and hear the plan for the weekend. The night ends with Scripture by the fire and quiet in camp.

Saturday

The hard day

Up before light for a climb to a high point by sunrise. After breakfast comes an hour alone with your Bible, a journal, and nothing else. Then real work: clearing trail, splitting wood, or a service project at the camp. Around the fire that night, a few people tell their stories.

Sunday morning

Send

Sunrise worship and communion outside. Each person writes one next step on a card and says it aloud to the group. Break camp, leave it cleaner than you found it, and head home by noon.

Bring

What to pack

  • Tent or hammock, sleeping bag rated for the season, and a pad
  • Broken-in boots, rain jacket, and warm layers
  • Headlamp, water bottle, and a small daypack for the climb
  • A paper Bible, a journal, and a pen
  • Any medication you need, and a list for the safety lead

Leaders keep one phone for emergencies. Everyone else’s stays in the box until Sunday.

Read this first

Safety

  • Everyone completes a medical form and signs an acknowledgment. Anyone under 18 comes with a parent or with written permission.
  • A safety lead with first-aid training, an emergency plan, and the authority to change anything.
  • The climb is sized for the least experienced person, with a shorter route that counts just as much.
  • Nobody is alone on the trail, and the solitary hour is within sight of camp.
  • We move or cancel for dangerous weather.

Contact and FAQ

Questions, answered straight

Is fasting safe?

For many healthy adults a modest fast is manageable, but it isn't right for everyone. Talk with your doctor first. Anyone under 18 takes part through the Prayer fast only. Some adults should choose it too, and the Water fast level requires a physician's written approval. Read Fast safely before you sign up.

I've never fasted. Can I join?

Yes. Start with the Prayer fast or One meal level. You'll read the same Scripture and sit in the same group as everyone else.

Can I fast from something other than food?

Yes. The Prayer fast level gives up a daily comfort such as your phone, social media, or television, and gives that time to prayer and Scripture. It is a full part of the fast, not a consolation prize.

What if I can't finish?

Then you stop, or you move to a gentler level, and you stay in your group. Come to the table on day 14 either way. We are not saved by finishing.

Does fasting earn favor with God?

No. We are accepted by God through faith in the finished work of Jesus Christ and nothing else. Fasting is a way to humble ourselves and seek Him, never a way to put Him in our debt.

Is this a weight-loss or detox program?

No. It is a spiritual discipline. We make no promises about physical results.

Do I need to belong to a certain church?

No. The fast is for any follower of Jesus, and what we believe is on the About page. We'd encourage you to tell your own pastor you're doing it.

Which Bible translation do you use?

The English Standard Version (ESV), printed in full on each day's page so you don't need anything else in your hands.

Shouldn't fasting be done in secret?

Jesus warned against fasting to be seen and admired (Matthew 6:16-18). Scripture also records God's people fasting together, as in Joel 2 and Acts 13. We fast together for accountability and keep it free of show: no rankings, no boasting, no before-and-after photos.

Where does the giving go?

Ten percent of every sale goes to hunger relief and church benevolence. See the Giving page for the ledger.

What are your shipping and return policies?

Orders ship within the United States in 3 to 7 business days. Unworn shirts can be returned or exchanged within 30 days. Workbooks can be returned unopened.

Policy

Privacy

What we collect. Your name, email, mailing address, and order details when you buy something or register for a fast, and whatever you write to us. Payment card details are handled by our payment processor and never reach our servers.

Health information. We don't ask for your medical history. If you choose the Water fast level, we keep your physician's approval letter only until that fast ends, then delete it.

How we use it. To fill orders, run the fast, answer you, and send the monthly dispatch if you subscribed. We don't sell or rent your information to anyone.

Stories. We publish a testimony only with your written permission, and we'll take it down whenever you ask.

Your choices. Unsubscribe from any email with one click. To see, correct, or delete what we hold about you, write to hello@waroncomfort.org.
